Best AI tools for Google Workspace users

Updated 18 Oct 2026 · Prices from vendor pages only · How we rank →

The short answer

Each of these tools connects to Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Meet or Google Calendar. Google Gemini comes first: low-cost entry tier and deep ties to Google storage, Search and creative tools. The cheapest verified paid plan here is Google Gemini Lower paid tier ($4.99/month). 8 of the 10 have a free plan we could confirm. Every price below is from the vendor’s own page, checked 16–18 Oct 2026.

Ranked shortlist

  1. Google Gemini: Low-cost entry tier and deep ties to Google storage, Search and creative tools. Plan: Lower paid tier: $4.99/month
    2x Free usage, 200 Google Flow credits
  2. ProWritingAid: Grammar and style checker aimed at fiction writers. Plan: Premium: $10/month yearly ($120) or $30 monthly
    Unlimited line editing reports
  3. Otter.ai: Meeting transcription and AI notes. Plan: Pro: $8.33/user/month annually or $16.99 monthly

    Unlimited AI note-taker, bot-free
  5. NotebookLM: Answers grounded in your own sources, free for personal use, with Audio and Video Overviews. Plan: In Google AI Pro: $19.99/month (Google AI Pro)
    4x limits, 300 sources
  6. Reclaim.ai: AI time-blocking for calendars. Plan: Not yet verified
  7. Apollo.io: B2B contact database and sales engagement with AI-written outreach. Plan: Not yet verified
  8. Gemini for Google Workspace: AI inside the Google Workspace apps teams use daily. Plan: Business Starter: $7/user/month
  9. Fireflies.ai: Unlimited transcription even on the free plan, across Zoom, Meet and Teams. Plan: Pro: $10/seat/month annual ($18 monthly)
  10. Fathom: Free AI meeting notetaker for Zoom, Meet and Teams. Plan: Team: $15/user/month annually or $19 monthly
    2 user minimum

Who should pick which

Pick Google Gemini for people already in Google's ecosystem (Gmail, Drive, Photos) (Lower paid tier: $4.99/month); think twice if you don't use Google services and want a standalone assistant. Pick ProWritingAid for fiction and long-form writers who want manuscript-level editing reports (Premium: $10/month yearly ($120) or $30 monthly). Watch out: monthly billing costs roughly three times the yearly rate. Pick Otter.ai for meeting transcription and notes for individuals and small teams (Pro: $8.33/user/month annually or $16.99 monthly). Watch out: promotional discounts were shown on the pricing page; check the current price at checkout. Pick Krisp for bot-free meeting notes and noise cancellation (Core: $8/user/month annually or $16 monthly). Watch out: no permanent free plan listed, only a 7-day trial. Pick NotebookLM for students and researchers working from their own documents (In Google AI Pro: $19.99/month (Google AI Pro)); think twice if you need open web search as the main source. Pick Reclaim.ai for calendar time-blocking for Google Calendar users (free plan: Lite: free forever). Watch out: paid prices not verified. Pick Apollo.io for prospecting database and outbound email (free plan: Free plan for light usage; Gmail only for sending). Watch out: paid prices not verified.

FAQ

Which of these is cheapest?

Google Gemini Lower paid tier ($4.99/month) is the lowest paid price we verified on this list (https://gemini.google/subscriptions/, checked 16 Oct 2026). Check the vendor page for tax, billing period and usage limits.

Which of these have a free plan?

Google Gemini: $0/month with a Google Account (Flash model, base usage limits); ProWritingAid: Free plan with core editing tools; Otter.ai: Basic: free forever; NotebookLM: Standard chat/generation limits, up to 50 sources per notebook; Reclaim.ai: Lite: free forever; Apollo.io: Free plan for light usage; Gmail only for sending; Fireflies.ai: Unlimited transcription, limited AI summaries, 400 mins storage/team, 20 AI credits; Fathom: Free forever: unlimited recordings and instant summaries.

How were these tools chosen?

They are the tools in ToolRank's rankings with a Google Workspace app listed as an integration, confirmed on the vendor's own website on 16–18 Oct 2026. Tools we could not verify are left out rather than guessed, and anything unconfirmed is marked Not yet verified.
